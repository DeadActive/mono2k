Introduction

MONO2K turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into a small Monomachine: six mono machines, a 64-step sequencer that locks any parameter to any step, and a lofi bus that breaks the sound on purpose.

Each of the six tracks plays one machine: a sine, two kinds of noise, a chip, three kinds of FM, a supersaw and a pulse, a chord, digital waves, a voice, and three FX machines that work on the track before them. Every track has the Monomachine's chain after its machine: an amp envelope, a band of filters, distortion, an EQ, rate reduction and a delay of its own, then MONO2K's bits, wavefold and XOR.

The sequencer is the Monomachine's, with the modern Elektron conditions: each step trigs the amp, the filter's envelope and the LFOs independently, carries locks, a chance or an A:B condition, and up to four ratchets.

Status and safety

What runs where today, and what installing will mean.

Today

MONO2K is a prototype. The firmware's C core, the same code that will run on the FM-1, runs in your browser on the emulator page, with the sound, the sequencer and the screen as they are on the device. Nothing has been flashed to an FM-1 yet: the FM-1 build adds Felucca's platform around this core (the hardware layer, USB, storage, the update loader and the installer).

Saves are kept in your browser and can be exported as project files. The save format may still change; older saves are converted as they load.

Installing

When the FM-1 build is ready it will install from the Installer page, over USB, in Chrome or Edge, as Felucca does. Installing other firmware is at your own risk. M-VAVE's own updater, or the installer's return to the official firmware, takes the FM-1 back to how it shipped.

Note

MONO2K is not affiliated with Elektron or M-VAVE. Monomachine is Elektron's; FM-1 is M-VAVE's. MONO2K's machines are written from scratch and measured against a Monomachine's output; none of Elektron's code or samples are in it.

The browser emulator

The FM-1 drawn in one bit, its screen, and the firmware running underneath.

Open the emulator and press Start to turn the sound on. Click the panel first so it takes the keyboard; Esc leaves it. Drag or scroll a knob to turn it. The emulator runs at 44.1 kHz, as the FM-1 does, and saves your work in the browser as you go.

MIDI and saving

The screen

The screen keeps one layout on every page. The header names the page, the track and its machine, and the pattern. The focus in the middle shows what you touch last: a value in big type, a machine's card, a held step. Under it, the step strip, and along the bottom the four knobs of the page in view, each with its value and its slab. The screen can be light or dark: GLO > SET, SCRN.

Pages

Eight pages, four knobs at a time. A button opens its page; pressed again it turns to the page's next four knobs.

The pages and their knobs

Tracks and machines

Six tracks, each one mono machine with its own sound, chain and steps.

Choosing a track and its machine

SELECT picks the track; in GRID, the upper keys T1–T6 do. ALGORITHM changes the track's machine: its card comes up, and its eight knobs start from the machine's own settings while the rest of the track (the chain, the LFOs, the steps) stays as it was. The machines come in the Monomachine's order, its three FM+ machines together.

Level, pitch and mute

On HOME, KNOB 1 and 2 are the track's level and pitch. In GRID the MUTE key mutes the track in view; GLO held with one of the six lower keys mutes that track from anywhere.

FX machines

FX-CHORUS, FX-RING and FX-DYNAMIX make no sound of their own: each takes the track before it (after that track's amp) and works on it. Put the source on track 3 and the FX on track 4; the FX hears the source before its VOL, so turn the source's VOL down to hear only the effect. The Monomachine's metal sounds are made this way: a plucky FM+PAR into FX-CHORUS with its feedback all the way up.

A sound, copied

On a track's page, GLO + REC copies the track's sound (its machine and every setting) and GLO + PLAY pastes it onto the track in view, in this pattern or another. On HOME the same two copy and paste the whole pattern.

Patterns and steps

Sixteen patterns a bank, each a whole song part with its own sounds; six tracks of up to 64 steps.

GRID

SEQ turns the keys into steps. The sixteen lower keys are the steps of the page in view; OCT− and OCT+ turn the page (four pages, 64 steps). A key on an empty step puts a trig there with the last note you played; a quick tap on a trig takes it away, with its locks.

  1. Press SEQ: GRID shows in the header.
  2. Pick the track with SELECT, or with the upper keys T1–T6.
  3. Tap the lower keys to place trigs. Hold one to edit it.
  4. Press PLAY.

Length and patterns

Hold one of the page keys P1–P4 and the pattern ends at the end of that page: 16, 32, 48 or 64 steps; GLO > TIME shows the length (LEN) to the step. PRESETS picks the pattern: at once when stopped, at the end of the bar while playing, so patterns chain as you play.

An accent plays the step harder (GLO > TIME's ACNT says how much); a slide glides into the next note without trigging again.

Parameter locks

Any knob, locked to any step: the Monomachine's way of making one track sound like many.

Hold a step in GRID and turn any of the four knobs, on any page: the value is locked to that step, and plays for that step alone. The knob shows the lock in reverse, and the step's cell turns checkered (with a condition as well, a mark in its corner). A step can carry locks on as many parameters as you like, up to the pattern's pool of locks.

Locks can change a machine's knobs, the chain, the LFOs, anything with a knob on a track's page. Taking the trig away takes its locks with it.

While recording live, a knob turned just after a trig plays locks its value to that step; between trigs it changes the sound itself.

To lock without trigging anything, give the step the trig type NONE: it moves the pitch and applies its locks, and restarts nothing.

Trig types

The Monomachine's magic: each step trigs the amp, the filter's envelope and the LFOs independently.

A synthesizer usually restarts all its envelopes on every note. On MONO2K, as on the Monomachine, a step can restart some of them and leave the others running: change the pitch under a filter sweep that keeps going, restart only the LFO for a new wobble, or move the note with no new attack at all.

Every type moves the pitch to the step's note and applies the step's locks. The machine's own envelopes (an FM+ operator falling from the trig, GND-SIN's pitch drop) restart with the amp, as on the Monomachine. Without AMP the note's gate runs on as it was: if the amp has already died away, a FILTER or LFO trig changes a sound you cannot hear. NONE is a trigless step: a pitch change and a set of locks, with nothing restarted.

Only the LFOs set to restart on a trig restart (LFO page, TRIG). Ratchets repeat the step with the same type. Notes played on the keys or over MIDI always trig ALL.

Ideas

  • A bass line with one long filter sweep: ALL on the first step, AMP on the others.
  • A held pad whose chord changes under one envelope: NONE on the chord changes.
  • A wobble that starts again on the off-beats: LFO on the steps between the notes.

Conditions and ratchets

After the modern Elektron sequencers: steps that play by chance, by loop, or on FILL; steps that repeat.

Conditions

Hold a step and turn PRESETS. The strip draws each condition as its own texture: a chance as its density of dither.

Ratchets

Hold a step and press OCT+ or OCT−: the step plays one to four hits, evenly through its length, each with the step's trig type. The strip marks the extra hits along the cell's foot.

Recording

Live while the pattern plays, or step by step while it is stopped.

Live

With the keys playing notes (not GRID), press PLAY and tap REC. Notes you play land on the nearest step; a hard key records an accent. Knobs turned while recording lock their value to the step just played. Tap REC again to stop recording.

Step entry

With the keys playing notes and the sequencer stopped, tap REC: STEP ENTRY. Each note you play is written to the next step and the cursor moves on. GLO + OCT+ leaves a rest, GLO + OCT− goes back a step. Tap REC to leave.

FILL

Hold REC while playing for FILL: steps with the FILL condition play, steps with !FIL rest.

The track chain

Every track has the Monomachine's chain after its machine, then MONO2K's destroy effects.

Amp

ATCK, HOLD, DEC and REL shape each note, on the Monomachine's measured curves: HOLD keeps the note at full level before it decays, so most sounds need no note-off; it counts in beats at the pattern's tempo (32 is a beat, 8 a sixteenth), as on the Monomachine. DIST drives the machine into a clip of its own, VOL sets the track's level into the chain, PAN places it, PORT glides between notes.

Filter

A high-pass and a low-pass as one band: BASE is the bottom of the band, WDTH its width (at the top, BASE alone is a high-pass; at the bottom, WDTH alone is a low-pass), HPQ and LPQ their resonance. The filter follows the note. Its envelope (ATCK, DEC) moves the base and the width by B-OF and W-OF.

FX

EQF and EQG are one band of EQ; SRR reduces the sample rate; the delay (DTIM, DSND, DFB, DBAS, DWID) is the track's own, in 8-bit mu-law. DSTR: BITS crushes, FOLD folds the wave over itself, XOR mangles the samples, VERB sends the track to the bus's reverb. MOD is a chorus, a flanger or a phaser across the stereo.

LFOs

Three per track, two pages each: TRCK and DEST say which track and parameter it moves (any track's), WAVE its shape, TRIG what a trig does to it (FREE runs on, TRIG starts it over, HOLD samples it until the next trig, ONE runs one cycle, HALF half a cycle), MULT and SPD its rate in time with the tempo, PHAS where it starts, DPTH how far, either way. An LFO on pitch moves it in coarse steps; for vibrato aim it at the machine's TUNE.

Arpeggiator

On ARP: MODE (OFF, UP, DOWN, U/D, RND, ORDR, the order you played them), SPD (1/32 to 1/4), RNG (one to four octaves) and GATE. Hold keys and it plays them in turn.

The bus

Where the six tracks meet: a reverb, a beat repeat, tape, ADPCM, hiss and a drive into the soft clip.

PageKnobs
TIMEBPM the tempo, SWNG the swing, LEN the pattern's length, ACNT how hard an accent plays.
GLITCHTAPE wow and flutter, ADPC a 4-bit ADPCM crunch, GLCH the chance of a beat repeat each step, SIZE the slice it repeats.
VERBDCAY and DAMP the SPU-style reverb at 22 kHz; HISS adds hiss and dust; DRIV drives the bus into its soft clip.
SETSCRN the screen light or dark; PROJ and LOAD pick a project and load it (turn LOAD twice).

The bus's settings belong to the pattern, so each part of a song can be worn differently.

Saving and projects

Four projects of four banks (A–D) of sixteen patterns.

Patterns

SAVE keeps the pattern as it is; GLO + SAVE goes back to the last saved version. What you play is your working copy: you can change it freely and keep it or not.

Projects

Hold SAVE to keep the whole project. GLO > SET picks a project (PROJ) and loads it (LOAD, turned twice). The first project, DEMO, holds the factory's bank A.
